## The finding that matters

**The streamed book briefly reads wrong, and the bot trades precisely then.**

3,467 samples over 20.6h: median `yes_err` **exactly 0.0000**, 75.5% exact,
unfillable **1.1%**. So the book is fine almost always. But it breaks in bursts —
33 episodes, longest ~60s, all seven coins.

Conditional sampling is the whole story:

| | median abs yes_err | unfillable |
|---|---|---|
| uniform random times | 0.0000 | **1.1%** |
| near a **filled** order | 0.0000 | 2.1% |
| near an **empty** order | **0.0400** | **10.5%** |

**9.5× enrichment.** A wrong book manufactures the apparent edge, the bot fires
on it, and the price it computed never existed — so nothing fills. Hourly
correlation of unfillable% against fill% is **−0.722**; hours under 2%
unfillable filled 81.8%, hours over 2% filled 16.4%.

This is STATUS.md's 8/24 line — *"the signal and the fill failure share a
cause"* — except the cause is the bot's own book, not market speed. It also
means the 39-miss streak on 8/31 **cost nothing**: those trades were never real.

**Root-cause candidate, NOT established:** `Book.seq` (`stream.py:61,66`) is
declared, initialised, and never assigned or read anywhere in the repo. Deltas
apply with no gap check and no resync. Real hole either way. The mechanism I
proposed for it — corruption accumulating over the window — was **tested and
refuted** (225 samples, error flat, correlation −0.142). Lag under load is the
other candidate and is not separable on current evidence.

**Instrumentation gap that let it run:** `book_age_ms` is FILE freshness, not
content freshness. It read 8–70ms and looked healthy through the entire episode.

## Proposed next step (not yet voted)

Errors are transient (<60s); a genuine dislocation persists. **Requiring an edge
to survive N consecutive ticks would filter manufactured edges without knowing
the cause.** Cause-agnostic, backtestable against `research/observations.jsonl`.
